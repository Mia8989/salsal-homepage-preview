Community screening & events

Screening finds what
a checkup misses.

SALSAL brings free health screenings and education directly into the communities most affected by diabetes-related wounds and peripheral artery disease, so people learn their risk before it becomes an emergency.

01
What to expect

Not a health fair. A full day built to catch PAD.

A SALSAL screening is not a one-table health fair. It is a full-day, professionally staffed event built around one goal: catching PAD and diabetes-related complications before they lead to amputation.

  • Patient screenings with referrals to local providers for follow-up care
  • Patient and family education on prevention
  • Services and support from industry and local sponsors
  • Provider roundtable discussions on best practices in limb preservation
  • Live case studies for attending clinicians
  • Hands-on involvement from local nursing students
  • Data collection to track improved outcomes over time
The community screening events were initiated as a way to support patients in need of wound care when most treatment options were closed during COVID. We found we were not only screening patients whose care had been interrupted, we were also seeing patients in need who had never been treated in the past. Carol Barry, SALSAL Board Member

This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider.

Who these events serve

Communities carrying the highest risk.

SALSAL prioritizes communities carrying the highest risk: high rates of diabetes and amputation, limited access to specialty vascular and wound care, and real barriers to getting there in the first place. Many people we screen are managing diabetes or PAD without steady access to a caregiver, reliable transportation, or the money to miss a day of work for a doctor's visit. A free screening removes several of those barriers at once and puts a trained provider in front of someone who might otherwise wait until a wound is already serious.
