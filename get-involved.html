Volunteer

SALSAL runs on volunteers

The Save A Leg, Save A Life Foundation runs on volunteers. Our Board of Directors serves as volunteers, and so does most of the team behind every screening, campaign, and event.

"When you volunteer in our organization, you are demonstrating love, hope, and compassion to our community members that may be facing unnecessary amputations."
Frank Aviles, Jr., Physical Therapist

Ways to volunteer

A

SALSAL Ambassador

Our biggest volunteer role. Ambassadors are well-connected in public health locally and help organize supporters, sponsors, and attendance for a screening event, starting months in advance. Resume required.

P

Event Promotion

Get the word out in your community so people actually show up to a free screening. No special credentials needed, just local reach and follow-through.

E

Event Production

Set up and staff the event itself. We typically draw production volunteers from nursing students and local healthcare practitioners.

B

Board Membership

Join the volunteer Board of Directors that governs SALSAL. Resume required.

Select at least one area of interest when you apply: producing or promoting a screening event, serving as an event ambassador, organizing a White Sock Campaign event, or board membership.

The White Sock Campaign

One sock. A conversation about limb loss.

Every September, PAD Awareness Month, SALSAL runs a grassroots awareness push called the White Sock Campaign. It puts peripheral artery disease, critical limb ischemia, and preventable lower extremity amputation in front of people who have never heard those terms before.

How it works

1. Wear a single white sock on an exposed leg, at home, at work, or out in public.
2. Photograph it and share on social media.
3. Tag it: @thesalsalorg #whitesockcampaign #PADawareness #salsal #savealegsavealife #PADAwarenessMonth #takeyoursocksoff #limbsalvage #amputationprevention

The single sock is deliberate. It draws the eye to the leg, and to a fact many people do not know: many patients living with PAD or diabetes cannot afford something as basic as a diabetic sock. Funds raised through the campaign go toward educational programs for the public and for healthcare providers.

Organizing a White Sock event at your clinic, hospital unit, or community group extends the campaign's reach well past a single post. Volunteers who take this on are one of the volunteer roles you can select when you apply above.

Sponsor an Event

Reach the communities that need it most

SALSAL's community screening and education events are held where the need is highest: communities with elevated amputation and diabetes rates, vulnerable and at-risk populations, and proximity to a university or hospital program already committed to improving outcomes there.

"The healthcare team, healthcare industry, patients and families must be on the same team, same goals; decreasing amputations."
Dr. M. Mark Melin

What sponsorship makes possible

Direct connection and impact with patients who need it most.
Reach into high-risk, underserved, multicultural populations.
A shared room for community members and the healthcare community.
Resolution of unmet healthcare need through hands-on service.
Goodwill that travels by word of mouth through trusted sources: family, church, physicians.
Support for both physicians and patients in the community.
Collaboration across public, private, and nonprofit partners.

Sponsorship types: financial sponsorship, in-kind sponsorship (such as equipment), or another form of support your organization can offer.

The Harold L. Johnson Memorial Scholarship

A promise kept, one certification at a time

SALSAL supports wound care certification through the Harold L. Johnson Memorial Scholarship, which funds healthcare providers pursuing credentials through the American Board of Wound Management (ABWM): Certified Wound Case Associate (CWCA), Certified Wound Specialist (CWS), or Certified Wound Specialist Physician (CWSP).

The scholarship carries a personal promise. As Dr. Desmond Bell recalls: "I promised Harold I wouldn't forget him. I pledged a scholarship would be created in his name."

Harold L. Johnson was a Vietnam veteran who lived with post-traumatic stress disorder from his military service. He devoted years to The Save A Leg, Save A Life Foundation, supporting fellow veterans and people with limb loss, and spoke publicly on local radio about his own experience with diabetes and peripheral arterial disease.

Established in 2018 by SALSAL and ABWM, the program awards up to ten certification exam scholarships a year. The ABWM Foundation Board of Trustees reviews applications during its board meetings, and preference goes to honorably discharged Armed Forces veterans who show exceptional character and a track record of volunteer service.
