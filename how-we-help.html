How we help

Four programs.
One goal.

Catch preventable amputations before they happen. Each program attacks the problem at a different point: the clinician who makes the call, the patient who needs supplies, the community that never got screened.

01
Provider education

The people making the call need the newest evidence.

SALSAL runs local screening events, networking conferences, and teaching opportunities that raise awareness of peripheral arterial disease, critical limb ischemia, and preventable lower extremity amputation among the clinicians who see at-risk patients first.

This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider.

02
Harold L. Johnson Memorial Scholarship

Every certified provider is one more chance to save a limb.

Administered by the American Board of Wound Management Foundation and funded by SALSAL, this scholarship helps healthcare providers earn their wound certification.

03
Patient assistance

The practical things that keep a wound from becoming surgery.

For patients actively working to prevent an amputation, SALSAL provides direct support: diabetic shoes, dressing supplies, knee walkers, and other wound-healing items.

This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider.

Program 04

We go where the screening never came.

Free screening and education events across the United States, reaching communities that face the steepest barriers to care: no caregiver support, financial constraints, no transportation, and no one to ask. Each event pairs patient screenings with professional development for local providers.

Who carries the work

Four working groups.

SALSAL is an all-volunteer foundation. Every board member sits on a working group, and each group reports at the monthly board meeting.

  • TOBA. Advances Time Out Before Amputation as hospital policy, and builds the partnerships and published evidence behind it.
  • Education. Runs the webinar program and builds the provider education curriculum and downloadable guides.
  • Screening. Plans and staffs the free community screening events.
  • Outreach. Grows supporters, sponsors, and the foundation's presence in the wound care community.
