Our mission

Fewer amputations. Better lives.

"To reduce the number of lower extremity amputations and improve the quality of life of our fellow citizens who are afflicted with wounds and complications from diabetes and peripheral arterial disease."

The Save A Leg, Save A Life Foundation is a nonprofit dedicated to that single goal: fewer amputations, better quality of life for the patients living with diabetic wounds and peripheral arterial disease (PAD).

We accomplish it through advanced, evidence-based methods: educating healthcare practitioners and patients, community outreach, philanthropy, and patient advocacy. Our key objective is to modernize hospital amputation policy, educate the practitioners making the call, and put patients in a position to advocate for themselves.

What drives the work

Three pillars, one mission

E

Educate

We inform the public and healthcare professionals about evidence-based measures to prevent or delay non-traumatic amputations.

A

Advocate

We support people with limb loss, and the populations at elevated amputation risk, through policy work and community engagement.

C

Communicate

We build better coordination among the healthcare professionals treating amputation-vulnerable patients, so critical information does not get lost between specialists.

Why we exist

Built to close a gap

Dr. Desmond Bell founded the Save A Leg, Save A Life Foundation in 2015. As a wound care specialist, he saw the same gap over and over: patients and caregivers did not know what resources existed for limb preservation, even as the technology to prevent amputation kept advancing. SALSAL was built to close that gap.

The foundation operates as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit. Leadership is made up of practicing medical professionals, wound care specialists, and advocates who volunteer their expertise because they have seen, firsthand, how many amputations did not have to happen.

Our philosophy

We call it "Prevention Through Action, Not Words." Awareness alone does not save a limb. SALSAL pursues engagement at the personal and community level, and we challenge the idea that amputation is ever an acceptable first response to a preventable wound.

The people setting the direction

SALSAL's Board of Directors brings together wound care physicians, nurse practitioners, researchers, and industry leaders who volunteer their time to advance limb preservation.
