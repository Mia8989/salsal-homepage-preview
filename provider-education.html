The problem

A gap sits between
the evidence and
the operating room.

Roughly half of lower extremity amputations in the United States are performed without prior vascular testing or exploration of limb-preserving options. Research indicates that preventing an amputation is more cost-effective than performing one and absorbing the costs that follow.

A gap exists between academia, the medical community, and the patient populations most at risk. Provider education is how we close it, one care team at a time.

Source: SALSAL provider education materials. This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice.

What we cover

Five focus areas, taught to the clinicians who see at-risk patients first.

Sessions are built for mixed care teams: primary care, podiatry, vascular, wound care, and nursing in the same room.

  • The necessity of vascular screening prior to amputation
  • Workflow, triaging, and specialty collaboration across the care team
  • Revascularization concepts and techniques
  • The economic case for amputation prevention
  • Case studies in wound care, intervention, and limb salvage

The SALSAL Challenge

Call a time-out.

The SALSAL Challenge asks a simple thing of every hospital: require vascular testing and further assessment before approving a lower extremity amputation.

The model is not new. It mirrors The Joint Commission's 2003 surgical time-out protocol, credited with reducing wrong-site and wrong-procedure surgeries. A vascular time-out before amputation applies the same logic: pause, confirm, and rule out a limb-preserving option before the procedure that cannot be undone.

One North Carolina hospital that adopted the policy reported a significant drop in amputation rates after the first year. SALSAL recommends that hospitals adopt the policy and track their own before-and-after data, so the evidence keeps building.

This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider.
