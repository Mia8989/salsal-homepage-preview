Time Out Before Amputation

One question, asked
before it is too late.

TOBA is a national initiative from The Save A Leg, Save A Life Foundation addressing one of the most preventable tragedies in modern healthcare: the loss of a limb without diagnostic evaluation. Despite decades of progress in vascular and wound care, SALSAL reports that up to 50% of non-traumatic lower-extremity amputations are performed without any prior vascular testing. In many cases, the amputation is the first intervention attempted.

This information is for educational purposes only and is not intended to replace professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider.

The proposal

Before any non-traumatic lower-extremity amputation, call a Time Out and confirm that appropriate vascular studies have been ordered and reviewed.

01
Why TOBA matters

Today the decision is often made by eye, not by evidence.

Current practice is frequently based on subjective observation, that the foot simply looks ischemic. TOBA makes the amputation decision evidence-based through ABI, duplex, CTA, or angiography, and turns a single clinician's judgment into a multidisciplinary, shared decision.

P

The problem

Thousands of amputations occur each year without objective vascular evaluation, and hospitals carry the cost, the readmissions, and the legal exposure that follow an avoidable amputation.

S

The solution

A standardized Time Out confirms perfusion is assessed before any irreversible procedure, using objective testing rather than visual assessment alone.

I

The impact

Fewer unnecessary amputations, preserved mobility and independence, lower long-term care costs, and shared accountability across the care team.

02
Proof of concept

Where a Time Out has already been tried, amputations went down.

Asheville, North Carolina. After implementing a TOBA-style policy, the hospital reported a significant decrease in amputation rates within one year.

Baylor University Medical Center. TOBA aligns with the multidisciplinary, data-driven amputation prevention model championed by Dr. Jayer Chung.

The SAGE Group. Economic analyses show that each major amputation avoided saves the healthcare system hundreds of thousands of dollars in lifetime costs.

Goals of the initiative

Make the Time Out standard practice, nationally.

01

National adoption

Promote TOBA as a standard hospital safety policy, alongside the surgical safety checks that are already routine.

02

Payor support

Engage CMS and private payors to support vascular testing before amputation as a recognized quality measure.

03

Society endorsement

Partner with professional societies including SVS, ACFAS, APMA, and APWCA to endorse TOBA as best practice.

04

Education

Educate patients, clinicians, and policymakers on the role of objective diagnostics in limb preservation.
